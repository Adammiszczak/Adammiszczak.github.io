GEO is not only about making pages retrievable. It is increasingly about making the brand understandable, credible and consistently represented across the wider web.

When we talk about GEO, much of the discussion still focuses on content.

1. Can an AI crawler access the page?
2. Can the content be retrieved?
3. Is the entity clearly described?
4. Is structured data correct?
5. Are products available through feeds?

Those questions remain important.

But I think another layer deserves much more attention:

**brand evidence.**

By “brand value” here, I do not mean the financial value of a brand. I mean the body of evidence that exists around it across the web.

1. What are people saying about the company?
2. How is it described by journalists, creators, customers and communities?
3. What do reviews say?
4. Which products is the brand associated with?
5. How is delivery described?
6. What do people say about support, quality, returns or reliability?
7. Which competitors is the brand compared with?

Those signals are not controlled entirely by the company’s own website. And that is exactly why they matter.

## AI does not only see what you say about yourself

Traditional SEO naturally concentrated a lot of effort on assets under our control:

- pages
- keywords
- internal links
- structured data
- backlinks

AI-driven discovery expands that information environment.

In its [generative AI optimization guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide), Google says its generative AI experiences can show what is being said about products and services across sources such as blogs, videos and forum discussions.

OpenAI provides an even clearer example in commerce.

According to OpenAI’s [Shopping with ChatGPT Search](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/11128490-shopping-with-chatgpt-search), ChatGPT Shopping can use first-party and third-party product information. It can show review summaries based on public reviews and consider information such as price, availability and quality when deciding which merchants or products to surface.

This does not mean reviews are a universal LLM ranking factor.

> It means that **third-party information can become part of the evidence used to construct an answer or recommendation.**

That is a meaningful change.

## Research increasingly connects brand presence with AI visibility

Industry studies are also producing an interesting pattern.

[Ahrefs reports analysing 75,000 brands](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-brand-visibility-correlations/) and finding that branded web mentions had strong correlations with visibility across ChatGPT, AI Mode and AI Overviews.

Its [more recent research reports analysing 10,000 brands](https://ahrefs.com/blog/mention-types-ai-visibility/) across 260 million non-branded AI prompts. Different types of off-site brand mentions again correlated with AI visibility, with Reddit mentions showing the strongest relationship in that particular dataset.

The important caveat should be repeated every time:

**correlation ≠ causation**

A company cannot manufacture 1,000 mentions and assume that AI visibility will increase by a predictable amount.

Brand size, existing demand, category, platform behaviour, retrieval systems and many other factors can influence the relationship.

The same [Google guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide) also warns against pursuing inauthentic mentions as a GEO tactic. So this is not a new loophole. It is a strategic signal.

## Is this the new link building?

I would be careful with that phrase.

1. Links still matter.
2. Citations still matter.
3. A third-party page linking directly to your site can provide traffic and help machines discover supporting evidence.

But GEO adds another dimension.

1. A brand can be **mentioned without being cited**.
2. A website can also be **cited without the brand being mentioned prominently in the answer**.

Those are different types of visibility, a distinction also explored in [Semrush’s Ghost Citations Study](https://www.semrush.com/blog/the-ghost-citations-study/).

That means the off-site objective can no longer be reduced to:

> Get a link from a strong domain.

I would expand it to:

> Build credible, consistent associations between the brand and the topics, products and qualities it wants to be known for.

That can happen through links.

But also through:

- editorial coverage
- customer reviews
- creator content
- communities and forums
- comparisons
- rankings and awards
- partnerships
- marketplace presence
- product data
- expert recommendations

This feels less like traditional link acquisition and more like **brand evidence engineering**.

## For e-commerce, the brand story becomes very concrete

This is especially interesting in commerce.

Imagine a user asks:

> What is a reliable store for running shoes with fast delivery and easy returns?

That question is not simply a keyword.

It contains several criteria:

- product relevance
- merchant reliability
- delivery
- returns
- customer experience

The company’s category page can explain all of those things.

1. But third-party sources may provide another version of the story.
2. Reviews might say delivery is consistently fast.
3. Or consistently late.
4. Communities might recommend the merchant.
5. Or warn users about support.
6. Product aggregators may show competitive pricing.
7. Merchant feeds may show current availability.
8. The AI system may encounter several of these evidence layers when producing an answer.

That makes GEO much more cross-functional.

## GEO increasingly touches the whole company

This is where I think the topic becomes strategically important.

* SEO cannot manufacture a strong brand reputation.
* GEO cannot fix a poor delivery experience.
* Structured data cannot erase bad reviews.
* A content team cannot compensate forever for inconsistent product information.

So a mature GEO program increasingly connects:

- **SEO / GEO:** discoverability, retrieval, content, measurement

- **Brand / PR:** awareness, credible mentions, expert coverage

- **Customer Experience:** reviews, support, satisfaction, reputation

- **Product / Merchandising:** accurate attributes, availability, assortment

- **Operations:** delivery, returns, fulfilment

- **Data:** consistent facts across systems

The goal is not to make all of these teams responsible for GEO.

The goal is to recognise that AI systems can reflect evidence created by all of them.

## What I would measure

If brand becomes part of the GEO system, prompt monitoring alone is not enough.

I would monitor:

- AI brand mentions
- AI citations
- AI Share of Voice
- topics associated with the brand
- third-party web mentions
- review volume and sentiment
- creator and community presence
- branded search demand
- AI referrals
- business outcomes

And, as always:

- mentions ≠ citations
- citations ≠ traffic
- traffic ≠ incremental revenue

The purpose is not to create another artificial attribution model.

It is to understand whether the brand is becoming more visible, better understood and more consistently represented across AI discovery systems.

## Conclusion

Traditional SEO taught us to optimize pages. GEO increasingly forces us to think about **entities and reputation beyond the page**.

* The website remains important.
* Technical SEO remains important.
* Links remain important.

But the wider web can now become part of the answer an AI system builds about your company.

That means reviews, mentions, comparisons, product information, creator coverage and real customer experience may all contribute to the environment in which AI systems understand and recommend brands.

I would not call brand mentions a proven AI ranking factor. And I would not call this the replacement for link building. I would call it an expansion of off-site SEO/GEO:

> **from building links to building credible brand evidence.**

In traditional search, we often optimized for the query. In GEO, we increasingly also need to optimize the **understanding of the brand itself.**
